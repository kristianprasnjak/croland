## Lesson 1
- Welcome to your first real Croatian lesson!
- Here's a secret: you already speak some Croatian. *Hotel*, *banana*, *čokolada*, *restoran* — these are all Croatian words, and you just read them perfectly.
- By the end of this lesson you'll describe houses, cars, the sea and your coffee — in Croatian.
- To say anything about anything, Croatian needs just one little word: **je** (*is*).
- Notice what's missing? No *the*, no *a*. Croatian skips articles entirely. You're welcome.
- Bravo! You just built your first Croatian sentences — with no articles to worry about and spelling you can always trust.

## Lesson 3
- So far you can *describe* the world: the coffee is good, the day is beautiful. Now you'll make things *happen* in it.
- And notice something: you often don't even need *ja*. The verb ending already tells everyone who's doing it. Efficient, right?
- The ending is the name tag: **-m** = me, **-š** = you, no extra ending = he/she. It works the same for *piti* (pijem, piješ, pije) and *jesti* (jedem, jedeš, jede) — the middle changes a bit, but the endings never lie.
- Sjajno! You can now say what you and others are doing — and you've cracked the verb-ending code that runs the whole language.

## Grammar 3
- In Lesson 3 your ear learned the endings. Here's the system behind them.
- **Drop the pronoun:** the ending already says who. *Pijem sok.* = *Ja pijem sok.* — both correct, the short one more natural. Keep the pronoun only for emphasis: *JA pijem sok* (me, not you).

## Lesson 4
- You can describe things, introduce people, and say what everyone's doing. But so far, your Croatian comes out one short sentence at a time.
- Today you get three tiny connector words — **i**, **a**, **ali** — and suddenly your sentences flow: *Tata kuha, a brat uči.* Dad cooks, and my brother studies.
- You'll practice them on the warmest topic there is: **obitelj** — the family.
- English uses "and" for the first two — Croatian is more precise. If the second part is a contrast-but-not-conflict, use **a**. If it's a real "but", use **ali**.
- Izvrsno! Your Croatian now flows in full, connected sentences — and you can talk about the whole family.

## Vocabulary 4

## Grammar 4
- The test: if English "and" could be replaced by "while/whereas", Croatian wants **a**. If it's a twist, **ali**.

## Lesson 5
- Today is a big day. You're going shopping at a Croatian market — and on the way, you'll unlock the thing Croatian is famous for: **cases**.
- Don't worry. Your first case changes exactly *one letter*. Watch:
- When a feminine word (ending in **-a**) becomes the *target* of an action — the thing you drink, buy, or love — its **-a** turns into **-u**. That's the whole rule. Let's go shopping.
- You just discovered it yourself: **-a → -u** when the word is on the receiving end of the action. Grammarians call this the *accusative case* — you can just call it the target form.
- Čestitamo! You just learned your first Croatian case — and survived. One letter changed, a whole grammar door opened.

## Vocabulary 5
- Every word here ends in **-a**, and every one of them turns that -a into **-u** the moment you drink it, eat it or buy it. Learn each one as a pair.

## Grammar 5
- **What a "case" is:** Croatian shows a word's *role* in the sentence by changing its ending.

## Lesson 6
- Last lesson you mastered the *-a → -u* trick for feminine words. Today you take the accusative to the city — and discover something charming about Croatian: it treats *living beings* differently from *things*.
- Things stay the same. People and animals get **-a**. Let's catch that tram.
- Two quirks to meet today: *pas* squeezes into **psa** (*Gledam psa*), and *policajac* drops a letter: **policajca**.
- Bravo! You now handle the accusative for *everything* in the singular — things, food, people and pets.

## Vocabulary 6
- Living masculine nouns, each shown with its target form. Five of them squeeze a letter out on the way: **policajac → policajca**, **kupac → kupca**, **pas → psa**.

## Grammar 6
- Croatian likes to mark *who can be affected* by an action. Things can't care; beings can.
- This is why Lesson 3 felt easy: every object there was a "no change" word. Sneaky, right?

## Lesson 7
- One coffee, one friend, one tram — so far, everything came one at a time. Today the world multiplies: *knjiga* becomes *knjige*, *učenik* becomes *učenici*, and verbs finally learn to say **we**, **you all** and **they**.
- We'll practice in the liveliest place possible: a Croatian school full of children and songs.
- One exception today: *dijete* → **djeca** (children). Some words are too important to follow rules.
- Sjajno! You can now talk about many things at once — and your verbs finally have their full present-tense family.

## Vocabulary 7
- Masculine nouns take **-i**. Watch the four where a **k** softens into **c** on the way: *učenik → učenici*.

## Grammar 7
- With this page your present tense is *complete* — all six persons — and nouns can finally come in groups.
- **The rebels.** Four words ignore everything above: *dijete → **djeca***, *brat → **braća***, *čovjek → **ljudi***, *riječ → **riječi***. Learn them as words, not rules.

## Lesson 8
- Summer is coming — *ljeto dolazi!* Time to make plans: the sea, ice cream, swimming every day...
- The Croatian future is refreshingly simple: take a tiny helper word (**ću** — "I will"), add the verb's dictionary form, and you're done. *Plivat ću!* — I will swim! No new endings to learn — the helpers are old friends in disguise.
- Recognize the rhythm? *ću–ćeš–će–ćemo–ćete–će* echoes *sam–si–je–smo–ste–su*.
- Odlično! Past lessons gave you the present; now the future is yours too. Your summer plans are officially in Croatian.

## Grammar 8
- Rule of thumb: the helper *never* stands first — it's shy, it always leans on another word.

## Lesson 19
- Here's Croatian's deepest secret, saved for near the end: almost every verb has a *twin*.
- One twin describes the *doing*, the other the *getting done*. English needs whole phrases for this — Croatian does it with a prefix. Once you see it, you'll see it everywhere.
- The tell-tale words: *cijeli dan / dugo / svaki dan* → process. *Napokon / odmah / za dvije minute* → done.
- Fantastično! You've cracked aspect — the concept that makes Slavic languages famous. You now hear the difference between *doing* and *done*.
